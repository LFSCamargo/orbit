Orbit 2.6.1 is out, and it's the last Orbit you'll need to download from the website. From
now on, new versions come to you: Orbit tells you when one is ready, shows you what changed,
and updates itself with one button.

It also finishes what 2.6.0 started. Two of its biggest fixes, the end of crackling audio
and 2.4 GHz controllers in Windows games, needed a new build of Orbit Wine to reach you.
That build is here, and Orbit now asks before it installs it.

## Updates from inside Orbit

When a new version is out, Orbit shows it right after it opens: the version you have, the
one that's available, and its release notes. Choose **Update and restart** and Orbit
downloads the update with a progress bar, installs it and opens again. Choose **Later** and
it asks next time you open Orbit. Nothing is ever installed without you saying so.

A few things it takes care of for you:

- **It never interrupts a game.** Orbit doesn't ask while you're playing, and an update can't
  start while a game is running.
- **Everything stays where it is.** Your library, games, saves, settings and Steam sign-in
  live outside the app, so an update never touches them.
- **It only installs real Orbit releases.** Every update is signed when it's published, and
  Orbit checks that signature before it installs anything.

<Callout title="How Orbit knows an update is genuine">
  Each release is signed with a private key that only the Orbit release process has. The
  matching public key is built into Orbit itself. Before installing, Orbit checks the
  download against that public key; if a single byte was changed, or the update was signed
  with any other key, Orbit refuses it. The release pipeline also checks every signature
  against the built-in key before it publishes, so a release Orbit would reject never goes
  out.
</Callout>

This is the one time you need to update the old way: download 2.6.1 from the
[download page](/download) and drag it over your current Orbit. After that, Orbit takes it
from here.

## No more crackling

In 2.6.0 we gave Windows games a deeper audio buffer. Under Rosetta, a game's audio thread
sometimes misses its 10 ms deadline; Wine only kept 30 ms of sound queued, so the buffer
ran dry and the game crackled and distorted. Orbit now asks for 60 ms, enough to ride out a
missed deadline at a latency you won't notice.

There was a catch: Wine only honours that setting with a small patch we added to Orbit Wine,
and the Orbit Wine most people had installed didn't include it yet. **Orbit Wine 26.3.0-2**
does, together with the controller bridge that lets 2.4 GHz receivers and XInput USB pads
like the 8BitDo Ultimate 2C reach your Windows games.

When you open Orbit 2.6.1, it offers the new build with its own release notes. Choose
**Download update** and it installs in the background; your games switch to it as soon as
it's ready, and every game keeps its saves. Prefer to stay where you are? Choose
**Keep 26.3.0** and Orbit won't ask about that build again.

## Choose your Orbit Wine

**Settings → Compatibility** has a new **Windows games runtime** row. It lists every Orbit
Wine build, the ones you have and the ones you can download, and the one your games use
right now.

- **Pick an installed build** and your games use it from their next launch.
- **Pick a newer build** and **Install** downloads it, prepares it and switches to it.
- **Leave it alone** and your games always use the newest build you have, so updates just
  apply.

If you choose an older build, Orbit keeps using it even when a newer one arrives, until you
pick again. The old **Compatibility layer** row is gone: games never actually used the Wine
versions it listed.

## Updating

Download Orbit 2.6.1 from the [download page](/download) and drag it over your current Orbit.
Open it, accept the Orbit Wine update, and you're set: the next version will show up inside
Orbit.

Every change is in the [2.6.1 changelog](/changelog), and the code is on
[GitHub](https://github.com/LFSCamargo/orbit).
